Terminology & Trading Guide (quick reference)
Plain-English meanings of the charts/levels/alerts on this page.
Full Terminology 📖
Net Gamma Curve (snapshot)
Cyan curve: net gamma exposure by strike (SPY proxy). Above 0 = more stabilizing; below 0 = more “move-amplifying”.
How to use: premium-selling (condors/flies/credit spreads) tends to work best when price is inside walls and gamma near spot is positive.
Key Levels (dashed lines)
Put Wall: big put positioning level (often support).
Call Wall: big call positioning level (often resistance).
Flip: transition level where behavior can change (chop ↔ trend).
Gamma Wall: largest positive gamma strike (often “stabilizing magnet”).
Alert Status (live)
Inside Walls: spot between Put/Call walls → usually more premium-seller friendly.
Above Call Wall: upside continuation/tail risk → condors/flies: manage call side.
Below Put Wall: downside continuation/tail risk → be careful with short puts.
Distances in parentheses show how far spot is from each level.
Gamma Over Time (derived)
Blue line: Total GEX ($M) over time (context).
Spot/Flip/Walls: the key levels moving over time.
Bottom bar strip: green=above Call Wall, red=below Put Wall, neutral=inside.
IV Skew (two charts)
Skew curve: puts vs calls IV% by moneyness (selected expiry). Puts rich = protection demand.
Term structure: ATM IV% across expiries + skew magnitude bars.
Intraday: tracks today’s ATM IV% and ATM skew (vol points) with an hours/minutes x-axis.
Hover tip: hover the skew curve to see exact moneyness + put/call IV at the cursor.
Reset intraday: clears today’s stored intraday IV/skew history (useful if you want a fresh session).
How to use: if skew steepens quickly, be conservative with short put premium (wider strikes / smaller size).
Full S&P 500 Treemap + True Breadth
Treemap: shows which parts of the index are leading/lagging today (color = daily % change).
Sector filter + search: isolate leadership (e.g., Tech) or find single tickers fast.
% Above 50D / 200D: “true breadth” across the full S&P 500 — confirms whether the trend is broad or narrow.
How to use: when breadth is weak (low % above 50/200), be more cautious selling tight premium (condors/flies) because index moves can be dominated by a few names.
SPX Greeks (proxy) — ATM + Nearby Strikes
Shows ATM Greeks (IV, Δ, Γ, V, Θ) and a short list of nearby strikes around spot.
Δ values beside Greeks are changes since the last refresh (quick “is risk rising?” read).
How to use: before entering an iron fly/condor, check if ATM IV and skew are rising (tail risk), and if gamma is increasing (position becomes more sensitive to spot changes). For credit spreads, rising IV can help premium, but rising skew can warn against short puts.
Best SPXW Options Table (0DTE + 5D)
What it is: a ranked trade planner that proposes defined‑risk SPXW setups (credit spreads / condors / debit spreads) with a simple when / how / risk plan.
How to use:
- Regime first: Inside Walls → range/mean‑reversion more likely (premium selling favored). Outside Walls → expansion/trend risk (avoid tight short premium).
- Vol next: rising ATM IV + steepening skew = tail risk → trade smaller/wider and be conservative with short puts.
- Pick the row: income rows for chop days; debit spreads for confirmed break/hold days.
When NOT to trade (short premium): above Call Wall or below Put Wall with momentum, or during rapid IV/skew expansion (headline regime). If you trade, prefer a small defined‑risk debit spread with your confirmation.
0DTE Projection (Seasonality, 5m)
What it is: an “expected path” for today based on historical 5‑minute behavior for the same weekday (SPX via SPY proxy).
Mean + bands: cyan = seasonality mean; green/red = typical range (P75/P25).
When to trade 0DTE (SPX):
- TRADE (best): price is inside the bands and reverting toward mean, while your gamma regime is Inside Walls / supportive → favors defined‑risk premium selling (conservative credit spreads / wider condors).
- CAUTION: price is near a band edge or skew/IV is rising fast → trade smaller and wider, avoid iron flies unless very stable.
- NO TRADE (short premium): price breaks outside bands and holds / accelerates → treat as trend/expansion day; if you trade, consider a debit spread only with your directional confirmation.
Practical rule: use this as context, not a signal. Combine with Put/Call Walls + Flip and your risk limits.
Strategy Suggestion (rules-based)
Uses: (1) inside/outside walls + (2) gamma sign near spot.
Trading translation: “TRADE” favors your premium-selling structures; “NO TRADE (for short premium)” means consider waiting or using a directional debit spread only with your entry signal.
Reminder: these tools describe the current options positioning regime; they’re best used with your normal confirmations (trend, support/resistance, time of day, risk limits).
