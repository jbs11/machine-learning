Regime — Direction Bias

Are conditions structurally bullish or bearish? Trade only in the direction the regime allows.

Loading…
Ichimoku Cloud
Tenkan / Kijun / Kumo
—
Loading…
The Kumo (cloud) defines the macro support/resistance zone. Price above a green cloud = strong bullish regime; below red cloud = bearish. Tenkan (9-period midpoint) crossing above Kijun (26-period midpoint) is a momentum confirmation signal — the "TK Cross." The Chikou Span (close plotted 26 bars back) confirms when it clears prior price action.

Rule: Only buy when price > cloud AND cloud is green AND Tenkan > Kijun. All three aligned = high-confidence regime.
EMA Stack
8 / 21 / 50 / 200
—
Loading…
When exponential moving averages are stacked in order (8 > 21 > 50 > 200 for bulls), price is in a healthy uptrend. The EMA 8/21 cross catches early momentum shifts (fast signal). The EMA 50 is the swing trader's anchor — dips to it in an uptrend are buy opportunities. The EMA 200 defines the multi-month regime — price above = structural bull market.

Rule: Full bull stack (8>21>50>200, price above all) = 2 regime points. Mixed = 0. Inverse stack = −2.
GEX Sign
Gamma Exposure Regime
—
Loading…
Positive GEX — dealers are net long gamma. They sell into rallies and buy dips, acting as a shock absorber. Volatility is suppressed; SPX tends to mean-revert toward the gamma wall. Ideal for premium selling and tight range-bound strategies.

Negative GEX (Short Gamma) — dealers must buy as price rises and sell as it falls — amplifying every move. Small triggers create outsized price swings. Directional momentum strategies and long premium are favored.

Rule: Positive GEX = regime support (sell premium). Negative GEX = amplified moves (buy premium, wider stops).

Trigger — Entry Timing

Wait for momentum confirmation before entering. The regime sets the direction; the trigger tells you when.

Loading…
MACD
12 / 26 / 9 · EMA-based momentum
—
Loading…
MACD = EMA(12) − EMA(26). The Signal line is EMA(9) of MACD. The histogram = MACD − Signal — rising histogram bars mean accelerating momentum.

Bullish trigger: MACD crosses above Signal AND histogram turns positive — especially powerful when crossing from below zero (confirms trend reversal, not just pullback). Bearish trigger: MACD crosses below Signal with histogram turning negative.

Key edge: A MACD crossover that occurs while price is above Ichimoku cloud and EMA stack = highest-confidence entry. MACD divergence (price makes new low but MACD does not) predicts reversal 2–3 sessions ahead.
RSI Divergence
14-period · Momentum oscillator
—
Loading…
RSI measures the speed of price change (0–100). Levels: Overbought >70, Neutral 40–60, Oversold <30.

Divergence (highest value): Bullish — price makes lower low but RSI makes higher low = buyers absorbing selling, reversal near. Bearish — price makes higher high but RSI makes lower high = exhaustion, reversal near.

Regime filter: In a bull regime, RSI > 50 is a confirmation that momentum is intact. RSI pulling back to 40–50 in an uptrend = buy-the-dip opportunity, not reversal. RSI < 40 in an uptrend = warning; wait for reclaim of 50 before entries.
Options Flow Spike
Call / Put volume & premium
—
Loading…
Unusual options activity reveals institutional positioning before price moves. A sudden spike in call volume + premium on SPY/SPX signals that large players are positioning for upside — often ahead of a catalyst. A spike in put volume + premium signals hedging or directional bearish bets.

Flow sentiment vs. price: When options flow turns bullish but price is still falling = smart money buying the dip. When flow turns bearish while price is rallying = distribution — institutional players selling into strength.

Trigger rule: A net-call flow spike in SPY (>2× average call volume) that aligns with MACD crossover = confirmed trigger. Use as "green light" to enter within the next 1–2 sessions.

Magnitude — Size the Trade

Regime and trigger tell you direction and timing. Magnitude tells you how large to trade and how wide to set stops.

Loading…
ATR — Average True Range
14-period · Daily range expectation
—
Loading…
ATR measures the average daily range of SPX over 14 sessions. It tells you how much the market typically moves — and therefore how wide stops must be to avoid random noise.

Stop sizing rule: Minimum stop = 1× ATR from entry (never tighter, or noise will stop you out). For 0DTE options, ATR/2 = expected intraday range each half-day.

Position sizing: Risk ÷ (ATR × contract delta) = number of contracts. Higher ATR = fewer contracts to maintain constant dollar risk. ATR expanding (rising) = market becoming more volatile; reduce size proactively. ATR contracting = volatility compression before a breakout — watch for regime shift.
VIX Level & IV Rank
Implied vol percentile · 52-week range
—
Loading…
VIX Level determines the overall volatility budget. High VIX = expensive options + wide swings = smaller position size and wider stops. Low VIX = cheap options + tight ranges = larger size, tighter stops.

IV Rank (IVR) = (Current VIX − 52w Low) ÷ (52w High − 52w Low) × 100. IVR >50 → options are relatively expensive → sell premium (iron condors, credit spreads). IVR <30 → options are cheap → buy premium (debit spreads, long straddles). IVR 30–50 → neutral → directional spreads at fair value.

Size rule: Position size × (1 − VIX/50). At VIX=25, use 50% of max size. At VIX=40, use 20%.
VIX Term Structure Slope
9D → 3M contango / backwardation
—
Loading…
The slope of the VIX curve from front (VIX9D) to back (VIX3M) reveals how fear is distributed across time.

Steep contango (VIX3M ≫ VIX9D): Near-term calm, longer-term uncertainty. Dealers are hedged. Premium selling is rewarded. Use full calculated position size.

Flat curve (VIX3M ≈ VIX9D): Market is uncertain across all timeframes. Use 75% size. Reduce short-dated premium exposure.

Backwardation (VIX9D > VIX3M): Immediate panic priced into front-month. Dealers must amplify short-term moves. Halve position size, widen stops by 50%, avoid selling premium on 0DTE/1DTE.

How to Use The EDGE — Step by Step

A repeatable pre-trade checklist. Run through all three layers before pressing any button.

Step 1 — Pre-market
🌡 Check Regime
Open this page before market open. Check Layer 1. Is price above or below the Ichimoku cloud? Is the EMA stack bullish, bearish, or mixed? What is the GEX sign for SPY?

If regime is bearish, only consider put spreads or short call spreads — do not fight the regime for directional long trades. If neutral (price in cloud), reduce all sizes by 50% and wait for clarity.
Step 2 — First 15 minutes
⚡ Wait for Trigger
Never enter at the open on direction alone. Wait for MACD histogram to turn in the direction of the regime. Confirm RSI is above 50 (bull) or below 50 (bear) and trending, not just touching. Check options flow for a confirming spike in the direction of your trade.

If all three trigger signals align with the regime within the first 30–60 minutes, that is your entry window.
Step 3 — Entry sizing
🎯 Apply Magnitude
Calculate max dollar risk for the trade. Divide by ATR (in points) to get raw position size. Apply VIX size multiplier: size × (1 − VIX/50). If term structure is in backwardation, halve the result again.

Set your stop at 1× ATR below entry (longs) or above (shorts). Never move stop against the position.
Step 4 — In the trade
📈 Monitor Layer Changes
Refresh this page every 30–60 minutes while in a trade. If the regime flips (price crosses cloud, EMA stack inverts), exit immediately — the structural case for your trade no longer exists.

If trigger signals weaken (MACD histogram shrinking, RSI losing 50) but regime is intact, reduce size by 50% — the trade may still work but with less conviction.
Step 5 — Exit rules
🚫 When to Get Out
Exit when: (a) stop hit (1× ATR), (b) regime flips, (c) trigger fires in opposite direction, (d) profit target reached (2× ATR from entry = 2:1 R/R minimum).

For 0DTE: exit by 3:30pm ET unless in strong profit — time decay accelerates in last 30 minutes and can eliminate gains instantly. For 1DTE/5DTE: hold only if regime and trigger remain aligned.
Bonus — Confluence
🌟 Maximum Confluence Setup
The highest-probability setups occur when all of these align simultaneously: Price above green Ichimoku cloud + full bull EMA stack + positive GEX + MACD bullish cross + RSI > 50 rising + net call flow spike + VIX < 20 + contango term structure + ATR contracting.

This setup appears 2–4 times per month. When it does, use full calculated size. These are the trades that build accounts.
⚠ Key Reminders
  • No single indicator is sufficient — the EDGE comes from confluence across all three layers. A perfect trigger in a bearish regime is still a losing setup.
  • GEX sign is the most underrated regime filter. Negative GEX (short gamma) means dealers amplify your losses AND your gains — it is not a directional signal on its own, but it multiplies magnitude.
  • RSI divergence is a leading signal (predicts reversal 2–3 sessions ahead). MACD crossover is a lagging confirmation. Use divergence to prepare, use MACD to pull the trigger.
  • ATR-based stops are not optional. They exist to survive market noise. A stop tighter than 1× ATR will be hit by random price action regardless of direction — you will be right on direction and still lose money.
  • VIX term structure backwardation is the single best signal that you should reduce size immediately. It indicates panic and dealer-amplified moves — the market will be bigger than your model expects.
  • All live data on this page is 15–20 minutes delayed via yfinance. For live 0DTE execution, always confirm entry signals on a real-time charting platform before placing the order.
