      How to trade with fundamentals + options

      Use fundamentals to choose what to trade and options structure to choose how to express the view — always with defined risk (spreads, not naked short options).

      Step 1 — Start on Overview

      Scan the universe table and charts. Sort mentally by sector filter if you care about one theme (e.g., Technology, Energy). Compare forward P/E (valuation) with EPS growth YoY (momentum). Toggle Options focus to overlay IV percentile — this tells you whether the options market is paying you well to sell premium or whether long-vol structures are cheap.

      Step 2 — Open Asset detail for your symbol

      Click any row or pick a symbol. Read the summary, EPS trend, and margin chart. Check IV percentile and the options impact box (vol bias, skew, favored structures). If fundamentals are bullish but IV is elevated, prefer bull call spreads or cash-secured puts over naked calls. If fundamentals are weak and IV is high, consider bear call spreads or wide iron condors with small size.

      Step 3 — Confirm with live fundamentals (optional)

      This dashboard uses a representative snapshot. Before sizing real trades, cross-check analyst targets, earnings dates, and composite scores on Fundamentals Analysis & Rankings (live API). Align ML signals and GEX from other site pages when available.

      Step 5 — Risk rules when mixing fundamentals + options

      • Defined risk only — spreads and condors cap loss; avoid naked short puts/calls on high-IV names (e.g., TSLA, NVDA) unless you are explicitly willing to own/short stock.
      • Earnings — IV often rises into prints; selling premium right before earnings without a plan is dangerous. Compare implied move to historical move.
      • Size — High IV names pay more premium but gap more; use smaller contract count than on SPY.
      • Confluence — Best trades combine fundamentals, directional bias on this page, acceptable IV regime, and (when possible) ML/GEX/flow agreement from other dashboard pages.
      • Indices vs singles — SPX/ES options reflect macro; single names reflect earnings and idiosyncratic skew.

      Step 6 — Use Sectors and Options playbook tabs

      Sectors compares average valuation and IV by group — useful for rotation (e.g., Energy vs Technology). Options playbook lists high-IV premium-selling candidates and low-IV debit/income names plus stocks trading below their 5-year average P/E.
