🔃 Contango vs Backwardation

▼ Contango (Normal)

VIX9D < VIX < VIX3M < VIX6M — fear increases with time horizon. Dealers are hedged. Market expects calm near-term with uncertainty further out. Premium sellers have edge. SPX tends to drift up.

▲ Backwardation (Fear)

VIX9D > VIX > VIX3M — short-term fear exceeds long-term. Market prices immediate shock risk. Options become very expensive. Mean-reversion setups emerge when VIX spikes and SPX overshoots support.

⚡ VIX Gamma Exposure — The Squeeze Mechanism
When VIX approaches negative GEX (Gamma Exposure) zones, options market-makers must hedge their books by buying volatility as it rises — creating a self-reinforcing gamma squeeze. Unlike equity GEX where dealers sell into rallies to hedge, VIX negative GEX means dealers amplify moves, pushing VIX (and SPX swings) dramatically higher with little friction.

Positive VIX GEX

Dealers are net long gamma on VIX options. They sell VIX as it rises and buy as it falls — dampening volatility spikes. SPX options become cheaper. Favors premium selling strategies on SPX.

Negative VIX GEX

Dealers are net short gamma on VIX options. They must buy VIX as it rises — adding fuel to volatility spikes. Small SPX moves can cascade into large VIX explosions. Avoid short premium; buy protection.

VIX Flip Level

The price at which VIX GEX transitions from positive to negative. When VIX crosses above its flip level, the gamma squeeze mechanism activates. Watch for VIX closes above key levels: 20, 25, and 35.

SPX / VIX Divergence

When SPX makes new lows but VIX fails to make new highs (divergence), selling exhaustion is near — a reliable 0DTE reversal signal. Conversely, SPX bouncing with VIX staying elevated = weak bounce, likely to retest lows.
